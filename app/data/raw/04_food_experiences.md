# Food and Local Experiences

## Hawker centres
Hawker centres are open-air/semi-covered food courts with dozens of independent
stalls and are central to Singaporean food culture. Well-known examples include
Maxwell Food Centre (near Chinatown), Lau Pa Sat (in the CBD, known for satay
street stalls at night), Tiong Bahru Market, and Old Airport Road Food Centre.
Most hawker centres are semi-outdoor but covered, so they remain usable in light
rain.

## Must-try local dishes
Chicken rice, laksa, chilli crab, char kway teow, satay, roti prata, and
kaya toast with soft-boiled eggs (a classic breakfast) are commonly recommended
local dishes.

## Little India and Kampong Glam food
Little India is known for South Indian and North Indian cuisine, including
banana-leaf rice meals. Kampong Glam has Middle Eastern and Malay food options
around Arab Street and Haji Lane.

## Night markets and pasar malam
Pop-up night markets (pasar malam) appear periodically around housing estates,
offering street snacks, games, and inexpensive goods; timings vary and are best
checked locally.

## Cafe and dessert culture
Tiong Bahru and Tanjong Pagar are known for independent cafes; Chinatown and
Katong/Joo Chiat are known for traditional Peranakan desserts such as kueh and
chendol.
